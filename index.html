INDUSTRIAL ARTS PROBLEMS

Our Chosen Problem

Our problem is in the learning area of "Industrial Arts" and it consists of the ''Severe shortages, outdated or broken equipment, and inadequate workshop spaces for hands-on subjects.''

Why is it a problem?

1. Severe Shortages - This refers to a critical prroblem where a lack of essential resources where the available supply falls dramatically short and causes accidents where people aren't quick enough to find a functinal tool.

2. Outdated or broken equipment - This refers to a problem where due to the poor maintenance of tools, the people using it might result to dangerous and fatal accidents because the tool might break or explode when used.

3. Inadequate workshop spaces for hands-on subjects - This refers to the restrict physical movement, increase safety hazards, and severely limit the practical learning experiences

What do we do to fix it?

  • Firstly, to fix the severe shortage problem, people should donate their leftover scrap metal, wood cuts or even extra wires. Working with local businesses gives you free materials that companies were going to throw away anyways. Studies by vocational groups like the Association for Career and Technical Education (ACTE). Show that utilizing these government grant programs, pumps billions into schools, keeping classrooms stocked so students can actually practice their trades.
  • Secondly, to fix the broken tool equipment problem, we need to do a weekly inspection on the maintenance of tools, to ensure that no tool is broken and that if there is a broken tool, it can easily be replaced. Bonus idea: Students should fund a VR headset and set up computer simulators, in that way students can learn how to weld, cook or run a machine digitally first while not worrying about the risks of accidents, it can also practice the safety of tool wielding by displaying warnings on when tools outdate.
  • Lastly, to fix the inadequate workshop spaces, we should have reserve square areas where each student has the exact materials and space that they need in order to complete their projects and ideas innovatively.

SUMMARY

The severe shortages, outdated equipment, and restricted workshop spaces in Industrial Arts programs create major safety hazards and limit practical learning. Lack of functional tools leads to accidents and delays, poorly maintained equipment risks breaking or exploding during use, and cramped spaces restrict movement while elevating safety risks. To solve material shortages, schools can collect scrap metal, wood, and wire donations from local businesses while securing vocational government grants through organizations like ACTE to fund supplies. Equipment safety can be improved through mandatory weekly maintenance inspections for tool replacement, alongside VR headsets and computer simulators that allow students to master trades safely before using physical machinery. Finally, workshop spaces can be optimized by designating structured, square work zones for each student, ensuring they have the exact space and materials needed to work safely and innovatively.
